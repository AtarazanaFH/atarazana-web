Málaga already has a port

You visit a summit. You come back to a shipyard.

Málaga stopped being a promise a long time ago. There are startups, universities, technical talent, capital, accelerators and a tech corridor that keeps on growing. That groundwork is already done, and it didn't happen by accident.

What we missed wasn't another banner or another agenda. It was a place where the people who build could get together regularly, without waiting for the next big event and without having to board a plane. Because an ecosystem doesn't live on its big dates, but on what happens in between: a conversation that wasn't planned, a prototype shown too early, a face that comes back, an idea people decide to build together.

La Atarazana fills that everyday gap. It's the shed that keeps all of it alive on the inside, at a cadence enough for faces to stop being strangers.

The work, not the talk

What matters here is what you build, not how you present it.

The shipyard is built around the people who do the work. What counts is what you're trying to build, what you learned trying, and how generously you share it. The title, the company and the follower count stay at the dock; they don't get past the door.

We like people who show what they do. Code, product, architecture, mistakes and uncomfortable questions: here a half-finished launch makes better conversation than a success story polished to perfection.

  • ▸ A demo is worth more than a pitch.
  • ▸ A hard decision is worth more than ten generic tips.
  • ▸ An honest question is worth more than a business card.

The house includes way more people than founders. Big companies are also built by engineers, researchers, product managers, designers and first employees who treat the problem as their own.

What matters isn't having started a company. It's building something real.

How the shipyard works

La Atarazana runs as numbered sessions, like launches that keep an archive. Each session brings together the formats that make sense for who's coming and what's worth showing, so no two nights are the same.

Launch

Real products running in front of the room, with the parts you don't usually see. If something breaks, it breaks in front of everyone, and that's usually the best part.

Below the waterline

Someone opens up an architecture, a product or a decision usually only discussed inside the team. The trade-offs, what it costs a month, and what was learned after the production incident.

Navigators' table

One table, few people, off the record. A real problem: finding a cofounder, building the first team, selling abroad, weathering a crisis or knowing when to change course.

Build nights

Laptops open, nobody presenting. You arrive with a task and leave with a commit, an experiment or a prototype.

The tide

The program ends and the conversation doesn't. Food, drinks, music and plenty of time for what an agenda can't program.

Each session runs the formats it needs. The ship changes; the craft stays the same.

Crew, not VIP

Curation depends on what you bring, not what you're worth.

The crew exists to protect what happens inside. We want every person who comes in to improve the conversation, and that's enough: in comes whoever is building something, and has something to give to the room.

We look for a deliberate mix of founders, engineers, researchers, product people, designers, exceptional students and profiles that are hard to classify. People with track record and people with no reputation yet who are already doing extraordinary work.

Each session keeps room for new faces. Belonging is built by showing up, coming back, and lending a hand.

Dock rules

Show the work

If you can show it, don't tell it with slides alone.

No theatre

Tables, demos and direct conversation. There's always room for the hard question.

Work over titles

What you've built matters more than how your role reads on your profile.

Unfinished belongs

Half-built work makes better conversation than a closed success story.

Give before you ask

Introduce two people. Share what you know. Unblock something.

Curated, not closed

We protect the quality of the room, and keep the door open to new talent.

What's said stays

Whoever shares decides what leaves the room.

Partners don't run the ship

Partners help the shipyard exist. They don't buy the stage, the crew or the conversation.

What we leave on shore

When a conversation needs a forced dynamic to get going, it probably was never going to be a good conversation. So the house sets up the conditions and steps aside.

  • ▸ One shared table.
  • ▸ An honest demo.
  • ▸ A beer after the last talk.

La Atarazana builds on the work of the people who've spent years growing Málaga's ecosystem, and adds another layer on top: more frequent, smaller, and centered on who builds.

Why Atarazana

Other tools, other products, the same hand.

We borrow the name for a reason. In Málaga's Atarazanas, the ships that crossed the Mediterranean were built: workshop work, no stage, people putting their hands on the timber and the iron.

It's that attitude we're interested in, not nostalgia: craft done by hand, experimentation, mixture, intensity and people who decide to create without asking permission. Today that energy lives in those building products, companies and technology from here. Only the material changed; the hand is still the same.

The shipyard exists when we work

The shipyard is the people who come back to work.

La Atarazana has no fixed home. The shipyard comes together each time we gather the right people in a room, and it comes apart when the night ends. What remains are the relationships, the products shown, the conversations left open and the things that start getting built afterwards.

We want to be the place you come back to: where a conversation becomes a company, a demo meets its first client and an idea finds its first engineer. See you inside.
